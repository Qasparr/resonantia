## How the written essay was rendered for the ear (the desk's stated rules)

1. The essay's words are preserved. Nothing is paraphrased, added, or
   cut from the argument. What changes is furniture, per the rules below.
2. Sections are announced ("Section One. The Frame."); each section's
   footnotes are read at that section's end, under "Footnotes to
   Section N", so the wells are heard whole without breaking the prose.
3. The § VIII ledger and the Annex ledger are read as their VERDICTS
   (their own closing paragraphs, whole), not as rows of letter
   arithmetic. The § X Table of Authorities is read as prose lines.
4. Parenthetical letter-by-letter breakdowns in body prose (e.g.
   "A1+X24+O15...") are rendered as the claim and its bank ("equals
   seventy-seven, in the English ordinal"); the arithmetic itself
   stands in the written ledgers.
5. Greek and Hebrew words are read in the transliterations the essay
   itself prints (πλοῖον -> ploion; σκαμνόν -> skamnon; ἀλήθεια ->
   aletheia; θέλημα -> thelema; πειρατής -> peirates; πεῖρα -> peira;
   עז -> Oz). The Greek commit hash in Annex note 2 is print furniture
   and is not spoken.
6. Load-bearing numbers are spelled out as words; "§" reads "Section";
   "Δ" reads "delta"; "==" reads "equals"; "Oz×Duty" reads "Oz times
   Duty"; display capitals are lowered to title case for the ear
   (TRVVTH reads "Truth"); HVLL / HULL / VVHY / XVHD are spelled
   letter-by-letter where the spelling itself is the argument.
7. The Colophon's Typography paragraph — a record about print faces —
   is page furniture and is not read; the Colophon's generation line,
   authorship, annex note, Flagpole, motto, and the Ratification are

## Chunk 08-s4-code — IV. THE CODE — Articles Before the Voyage
Section Four. The Code. Articles Before the Voyage.

Here is the fact the whole essay turns on, and it is a fact of record, not of romance: before the voyage, the pirates wrote their law down. Not after the taking, not at the trial: before. Exquemelin, who sailed with the buccaneers and printed their customs in The Buccaneers of America, sixteen seventy-eight, describes the council that agreed certain articles, which are put in writing, by way of bond or obligation, which every one is bound to observe, and names the fund from which every payment flows: the common stock of what is gotten by the whole expedition, for otherwise it is the same law among these people as with other Pirates: No prey, no pay. The articles fix, in advance, what the captain draws for his ship, what the carpenter and the surgeon are paid, and what a wound is worth, in Exquemelin's scale, six hundred pieces of eight for a right arm, five hundred for a left arm or a right leg, four hundred for a left leg, one hundred for an eye, one hundred for a finger, every sum taken out of the capital sum or common stock of what is gotten by their piracy, and only then a very exact and equal dividend is made of the remainder among them all.

Bartholomew Roberts' articles, printed by Captain Charles Johnson in A General History of the Pyrates, seventeen twenty-four, run the same law two generations later, and three of them carry this essay.

Article One: Every man has a vote in affairs of moment; has equal title to the fresh provisions, or strong liquors, at any time seized, and may use them at pleasure, unless a scarcity makes it necessary, for the good of all, to vote a retrenchment.

Article Ten: The Captain and Quartermaster to receive two shares of a prize: the master, boatswain, and gunner, one share and a half, and other officers one and quarter.

Article Nine: No man to talk of breaking up their way of living, till each had shared one thousand pounds. If in order to this, any man should lose a limb, or become a cripple in their service, he was to have eight hundred dollars, out of the public stock, and for lesser hurts, proportionately.

Read them as a constitution, because that is what they are. The right is never naked: a share exists because the articles created it, a vote exists because the articles swear it, compensation exists because the common stock is charged with it before any dividend moves. Every right in the code is multiplied by its duty: the duty to sail, to fight, to keep the articles, to take no prey and therefore draw no pay. This is Oz times Duty, the founder's handshake clause, written in sixteen seventy-eight and seventeen twenty-four by men who had never heard his name: no agreement may sever duty from right at any price. And on Roberts' ship, the captain who held two shares held them by the same paper that gave the cabin boy his half-share and the cripple his eight hundred. The captain was chosen by the crew's vote and answerable to it; the articles, not the man, were sovereign. Roberts himself, as Johnson reports him, weighed the bargain in the open, plenty, ease, liberty and power on one side, a sour look or two at choking on the other, and gave his verdict as his motto: a merry life and a short one shall be my motto. A man may refuse that bargain. He may not call it lawless. It is more law, sworn earlier and kept closer, than most licensed ships ever carried.

## Chunk 10-s5-license — V. THE LICENSE — The Same Act, the Other Paper
Section Five. The License. The Same Act, the Other Paper.

If the code is a constitution, the license is its counterfeit, and the difference between the pirate and the privateer is the cleanest proof in the law that authority, as the world practices it, is a paper relation and not a moral one. The United States Constitution keeps the two doors in one corridor: Article One, Section Eight gives Congress power to define and punish Piracies and Felonies committed on the High Seas, clause ten, and, in the very next breath, power to grant Letters of Marque and Reprisal, clause eleven. Same section, same sea, same acts of taking: one clause to hang the taker, one clause to commission him. A letter of marque does not change what the privateer does. It changes what he is called, and what he is called determines whether the identical rope is a prize crew's tool or a noose.

The Supreme Court's own record supplies the demonstration, in facts no novelist would dare file. In United States versus Smith, eighteen U.S., five Wheaton, one fifty-three, eighteen twenty, the prisoner had sailed in the Creollo, a private armed vessel commissioned by Buenos Ayres, a licensed man, doing licensed taking in a war against Spain. The crew mutinied, seized a second commissioned vessel, the Irresistible, in port, appointed their officers, proceeded to sea on a cruise without any documents or commission whatever, and plundered a Spanish vessel on the high seas. Justice Story, for the Court, held the definition sufficient and the act piracy: piracy, by the law of nations, is robbery upon the sea. Read the special verdict slowly. The men did not change. The seamanship did not change. The plunder did not change. What changed was the paper: the commission was gone, and with the commission gone, the same hands doing the same work crossed from privateering into piracy. The law itself testifies that the line is the license. The act is one; the paper is the difference; and a difference made entirely of paper is a difference made by whoever issues paper.

Rome saw the pirate from the other side of the same door. Cicero, De Officiis, book three: nam pirata non est ex perduellium numero definitus, sed communis hostis omnium: a pirate is not included in the number of lawful enemies, but is the common foe of all the world; and with him there ought not to be any pledged word nor any oath mutually binding, in the Miller translation. The later admiralty tag, hostis humani generis, enemy of all mankind, descends from this passage, and the Truth audit, Section Nine, prints the distinction the tradition often blurs: Cicero wrote communis hostis omnium; hostis humani generis is the later paraphrase, not Cicero's words. The man outside every license is placed outside every oath: no pledged word binds toward him, because he holds no paper from the world of pledged words. That is the true content of the sentence. The pirate is not condemned for the taking: states took, and licensed taking, on every sea. He is condemned for taking without leave, under a law he swore himself, in a company that answered to its own articles.

Against the whole architecture of leave and license, the founder's line stands as the essay's verdict, quoted verbatim from his own hand: Absolute Authority is not attained by license or permit but through Truth alone! The articles are the historical form of that sentence. A crew that writes its law before the voyage, fixes its shares before the prize, charges its common stock with its wounded before its dividends, and holds its captain by election and its every man by vote, has not asked any Congress for a letter of marque, and has not taken lawlessly either. It has done the third thing the corridor's two doors pretend does not exist: it has founded its authority in the open, on paper it wrote itself, sworn by every hand it binds. His filed reading of the film Pirates of the Caribbean belongs here, cited as his reading: the films read, in his judgment, as a documentary of the de facto government holding its people prisoners as property, the licensed world drawn from inside its own story, where the gallows and the commission hang in the same port. And the charter the code answers to is the one numbered by the frame: Liber Oz, Liber Seventy-Seven, which lists the rights of man as claims of the Living Man: asserted, not granted; held, not licensed. The pirate of the articles and the Living Man of the charter are the same figure at two dates: the one who will not wait for paper to be a person.
