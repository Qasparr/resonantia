Annex. The Greek Proofs. Annexed the sixteenth of November, twenty twenty-six, at the founder's order.

This annex is an appended emendation, in the founder's correctio discipline. The essay was delivered complete on the night of its building, fifteen pages, ten sections, and Sections One to Ten stand as delivered, byte for byte; nothing in them has been altered, struck, or smoothed. What follows was ordered after delivery. At the twenty-third hour, fourth minute, Eastern, the founder asked: Is there any Greek Expressions in any of this to act as Proofs? The honest answer was none: Section Eight's ledger ran on the Latin ordinal, the Agrippa bank, and the Hebrew, the Greek tongue, in whose bank this house's essay proofs are finally judged, acted nowhere in the essay. At the twenty-third hour, ninth minute, Eastern, he ordered the remedy: Fix the Greek bank, and annex the Greek proofs. So the Greek tongue was put to the essay's material. It answered, chiefly in the Agrippa register, as the findings below record, and its answers are annexed here, dated and marked, each correspondence carrying its reasoning beside its seat, as every correspondence in this house must. The weighings were run on the repaired bank, the defect and its repair are recorded at full size at the end of this annex, and the annex ledger is generated from the engine's output, as Section Eight's was.

The vessel: ploion equals three hundred ten. Ploion is the Greek word for a vessel, a ship. Reasoning for the seat: Section Three weighed the ship by her Latin letters and found the hull is the engine, fifty-four. The Greek tongue names not the hull but the whole vessel, and it lands in the oldest bank the house keeps: three hundred ten in isopsephy, which is Stool's weight in Agrippa, and Loots' with it: the seat and the take of Section Two, in the same register. The ship answers the stool across the tongues: what carries the booty weighs what the booty's seat weighs. The Greek weighing is Attested, as is each Agrippa weighing it meets; the bridge, the claim that the agreement carries, is labeled Derived: derived by the desk from two attested banks, standing for the founder's red pen as every bridge stands until he seats it.

The stool: skamnon equals four hundred thirty-one. Skamnon is Greek's own word for a stool, a bench. Reasoning for the seat: the first filing of the night was the founder's definition of the stool, and Section Two weighed Stool in its own tongue. Asked for its stool-words, the Greek bank returns four hundred thirty-one, which is Axoneme's weight in Agrippa: the bridge-word of the house itself, the keystone of Section One's first trinity. The bench of the Greeks and the axoneme meet in the oldest bank. The seat the whole frame turns on was already weighed in Greek; it waited only for the question. Tier as the vessel's: the weighings Attested, the bridge Derived.
