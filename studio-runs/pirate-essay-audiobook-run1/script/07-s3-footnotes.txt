Footnotes to Section Three.

One. Qur'an: Surat al-Kahf, chapter eighteen, verse seventy-nine, Sahih International translation: As for the ship, it belonged to poor people working at sea. So I intended to cause defect in it, as there was after them a king who seized every good ship by force. The hole as an act of preservation: the essay's sharpest single proof, and it comes from the well, not from the desk.

Two. I Ching: Hexagram fifty-nine, Huan, Dispersion, wind over water; the Judgment furthers the crossing of the great water, quoted at Section One. The ship is the instrument of the crossing in both wells.

Three. Tarot: The Six of Swords in Waite's Pictorial Key: a ferryman carrying passengers in his punt to the further shore, passage over water in a small, laden boat, the hull as the means of getting a whole company across.

Four. Classical maxim: Navigare necesse est, vivere non est necesse. To sail is necessary; to live is not necessary. The words Plutarch gives Pompey the Great, embarking the grain fleet in a storm, Life of Pompey, fifty. The ship outranks the shore's safety; Section Four shows what law the sailors wrote for themselves once aboard.
