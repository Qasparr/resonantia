Section Five. The License. The Same Act, the Other Paper.

If the code is a constitution, the license is its counterfeit, and the difference between the pirate and the privateer is the cleanest proof in the law that authority, as the world practices it, is a paper relation and not a moral one. The United States Constitution keeps the two doors in one corridor: Article One, Section Eight gives Congress power to define and punish Piracies and Felonies committed on the High Seas, clause ten, and, in the very next breath, power to grant Letters of Marque and Reprisal, clause eleven. Same section, same sea, same acts of taking: one clause to hang the taker, one clause to commission him. A letter of marque does not change what the privateer does. It changes what he is called, and what he is called determines whether the identical rope is a prize crew's tool or a noose.

The Supreme Court's own record supplies the demonstration, in facts no novelist would dare file. In United States versus Smith, eighteen U.S., five Wheaton, one fifty-three, eighteen twenty, the prisoner had sailed in the Creollo, a private armed vessel commissioned by Buenos Ayres, a licensed man, doing licensed taking in a war against Spain. The crew mutinied, seized a second commissioned vessel, the Irresistible, in port, appointed their officers, proceeded to sea on a cruise without any documents or commission whatever, and plundered a Spanish vessel on the high seas. Justice Story, for the Court, held the definition sufficient and the act piracy: piracy, by the law of nations, is robbery upon the sea. Read the special verdict slowly. The men did not change. The seamanship did not change. The plunder did not change. What changed was the paper: the commission was gone, and with the commission gone, the same hands doing the same work crossed from privateering into piracy. The law itself testifies that the line is the license. The act is one; the paper is the difference; and a difference made entirely of paper is a difference made by whoever issues paper.

Rome saw the pirate from the other side of the same door. Cicero, De Officiis, book three: nam pirata non est ex perduellium numero definitus, sed communis hostis omnium: a pirate is not included in the number of lawful enemies, but is the common foe of all the world; and with him there ought not to be any pledged word nor any oath mutually binding, in the Miller translation. The later admiralty tag, hostis humani generis, enemy of all mankind, descends from this passage, and the Truth audit, Section Nine, prints the distinction the tradition often blurs: Cicero wrote communis hostis omnium; hostis humani generis is the later paraphrase, not Cicero's words. The man outside every license is placed outside every oath: no pledged word binds toward him, because he holds no paper from the world of pledged words. That is the true content of the sentence. The pirate is not condemned for the taking: states took, and licensed taking, on every sea. He is condemned for taking without leave, under a law he swore himself, in a company that answered to its own articles.

Against the whole architecture of leave and license, the founder's line stands as the essay's verdict, quoted verbatim from his own hand: Absolute Authority is not attained by license or permit but through Truth alone! The articles are the historical form of that sentence. A crew that writes its law before the voyage, fixes its shares before the prize, charges its common stock with its wounded before its dividends, and holds its captain by election and its every man by vote, has not asked any Congress for a letter of marque, and has not taken lawlessly either. It has done the third thing the corridor's two doors pretend does not exist: it has founded its authority in the open, on paper it wrote itself, sworn by every hand it binds. His filed reading of the film Pirates of the Caribbean belongs here, cited as his reading: the films read, in his judgment, as a documentary of the de facto government holding its people prisoners as property, the licensed world drawn from inside its own story, where the gallows and the commission hang in the same port. And the charter the code answers to is the one numbered by the frame: Liber Oz, Liber Seventy-Seven, which lists the rights of man as claims of the Living Man: asserted, not granted; held, not licensed. The pirate of the articles and the Living Man of the charter are the same figure at two dates: the one who will not wait for paper to be a person.
