Section Four. The Code. Articles Before the Voyage.

Here is the fact the whole essay turns on, and it is a fact of record, not of romance: before the voyage, the pirates wrote their law down. Not after the taking, not at the trial: before. Exquemelin, who sailed with the buccaneers and printed their customs in The Buccaneers of America, sixteen seventy-eight, describes the council that agreed certain articles, which are put in writing, by way of bond or obligation, which every one is bound to observe, and names the fund from which every payment flows: the common stock of what is gotten by the whole expedition, for otherwise it is the same law among these people as with other Pirates: No prey, no pay. The articles fix, in advance, what the captain draws for his ship, what the carpenter and the surgeon are paid, and what a wound is worth, in Exquemelin's scale, six hundred pieces of eight for a right arm, five hundred for a left arm or a right leg, four hundred for a left leg, one hundred for an eye, one hundred for a finger, every sum taken out of the capital sum or common stock of what is gotten by their piracy, and only then a very exact and equal dividend is made of the remainder among them all.

Bartholomew Roberts' articles, printed by Captain Charles Johnson in A General History of the Pyrates, seventeen twenty-four, run the same law two generations later, and three of them carry this essay.

Article One: Every man has a vote in affairs of moment; has equal title to the fresh provisions, or strong liquors, at any time seized, and may use them at pleasure, unless a scarcity makes it necessary, for the good of all, to vote a retrenchment.

Article Ten: The Captain and Quartermaster to receive two shares of a prize: the master, boatswain, and gunner, one share and a half, and other officers one and quarter.

Article Nine: No man to talk of breaking up their way of living, till each had shared one thousand pounds. If in order to this, any man should lose a limb, or become a cripple in their service, he was to have eight hundred dollars, out of the public stock, and for lesser hurts, proportionately.

Read them as a constitution, because that is what they are. The right is never naked: a share exists because the articles created it, a vote exists because the articles swear it, compensation exists because the common stock is charged with it before any dividend moves. Every right in the code is multiplied by its duty: the duty to sail, to fight, to keep the articles, to take no prey and therefore draw no pay. This is Oz times Duty, the founder's handshake clause, written in sixteen seventy-eight and seventeen twenty-four by men who had never heard his name: no agreement may sever duty from right at any price. And on Roberts' ship, the captain who held two shares held them by the same paper that gave the cabin boy his half-share and the cripple his eight hundred. The captain was chosen by the crew's vote and answerable to it; the articles, not the man, were sovereign. Roberts himself, as Johnson reports him, weighed the bargain in the open, plenty, ease, liberty and power on one side, a sour look or two at choking on the other, and gave his verdict as his motto: a merry life and a short one shall be my motto. A man may refuse that bargain. He may not call it lawless. It is more law, sworn earlier and kept closer, than most licensed ships ever carried.

Now the weighing that seats this section in the frame. Disciple equals seventy-seven stands in the Third Trinity. Beside it, printed at full size, the miss: Master equals seventy-six, delta one short of the seat. The word for the one who would own the teaching falls one short of the number of strength; the word for the one who follows the teaching stands on it. The founder's position on the master-apprentice model is a matter of his filed record and is stated here once, soberly, in his terms: as a minor he sought legal emancipation and was refused, becoming a ward of the state, an experience he files as enslavement by the state, and the root of his refusal to submit to any master since; the model, in his judgment, is too corrupted to kneel to, and the work is done without one. The numbers were not arranged to preach that doctrine. They were re-weighed, and they preach it anyway: the code has no master in it. It has articles, shares, and a crew.

The code's own words weigh in, one after another. Shares equals seventy, and Lilith equals seventy: the divided thing itself weighs the Mother's number in the founder's audited ledger, the dividend and the first seat of his forty-seven Trinity's house at one value. Crew equals forty-nine, joining Green equals forty-nine, the house colour canon's third, and Sigma equals forty-nine, the mark his masters carry: the company, the colour, and the seal at one seat. And the smallest proof in the essay, printed with both of its banks open: Sail equals forty-one, and Oz equals forty-one in the ordinal bank, while Oz, the same word in its own tongue, weighs seventy-seven, Section One. Neither bank is hidden and neither is forced. The bridge doctrine has always said it plainly: every word is weighed in its own tongue first, and the bridge is the number two honest banks arrive at. Here, the word Oz in Latin letters gives forty-one, the word in Hebrew letters gives seventy-seven, and the difference between the two is not an error in either. It is the distance the bridge was built to span. To sail, spelled out, weighs what the name weighs in letters; to be Oz, in the tongue that coined the strength, weighs the frame. The crew sails under both numbers, and the articles hold them together.
