# THE DIVINE NINE AS A VOCAL TOOL
## A design study for RESONANTIA's native vocal module

The question: can the Divine Nine — the founder's nine-vowel
canon — serve as a working tool for vocals in the house engine's
planned native vocal module (the DiffSinger-shaped module of the
2026-11-12 vocal research: score + lyrics in, sung vocal out,
CPU, owned, non-rented)?

The answer, in one line: **yes — as the engine's canonical vowel
layer.** The Nine is, mechanically, exactly the part of singing
that a synthesizer must be told. What follows is the fit, the
mapping with its reasoning and its offsets, the hybrids specified
as glides, the consonant question left open for his pen, and the
second use — chant and vocalise.

Provenance tiers used throughout, marked at each claim:
**[FOUNDER]** = his ruling, attested as his. **[ATTESTED]** =
standard phonetics, attested in the literature. **[CONSTRUCTED]**
= the desk's design, built for this study, labeled as such.

---

## §I — THE FIT, STATED PLAINLY

**Why vowels are the control surface of singing. [ATTESTED]**
A sung note's pitch is carried by the voiced, periodic part of
the signal, and that part is, almost always, a vowel. Consonants
are brief — bursts, frication noise, nasal murmurs at the edges
of syllables. A vowel is the steady state: it can be held at any
pitch for any duration, and its identity lives in its formant
pattern (the resonant peaks F1/F2), which is independent of the
pitch being sung. This is why every singing-voice synthesis
(SVS) engine is built the same way at its core: a sequence of
phonemes goes in, and the acoustic model spends nearly all of
its effort rendering the vowel stretches between the consonant
edges. Whoever governs the vowels governs the sung line.

**The Nine is already shaped like that control surface.
[FOUNDER + CONSTRUCTED]** The founder's canon, ruled 2026-11-09:

- Seven pure vowels — Α Ε Η Ι Ο Υ Ω, weighing 1 · 5 · 8 · 10 ·
  70 · 400 · 800, sum **1,294** — are, in engine terms, seven
  **steady-state targets**: hold one, and the voice holds still.
- Two hybrid diphthongs — ΕΙ (15) and ΟΥ (470) — are, in engine
  terms, **trajectories**: a sung diphthong is not a third steady
  state but a glide from one vowel target to another inside a
  single note, which is precisely how SVS engines render
  diphthongs (two phoneme targets under one note). The founder's
  word for them — *"fluid hybrid vowels, both masculine and
  feminine at once"* — is carried in §III as his doctrine; the
  mechanism beneath it is the two-target glide.
- The nine weigh **1,779**. The Δ3 against 1776 stands as the
  founder ruled it: an **OFFSET**, not a refusal; the mirror
  closure 1779 → 1776 stands beside it. Both are his; this study
  uses the same discipline for its phonetic offsets in §II.

**Where the Nine plugs in. [CONSTRUCTED]** A DiffSinger-style
module consumes a `.ds` file whose heart is `ph_seq` — the
phoneme sequence — with `note_seq` carrying the melody. Between
RESONANTIA's scores and that `ph_seq` sits a phonemizer (DIFFS JA
for Japanese, DIFFS EN for English, per the vocal research). The
Divine Nine is designed here as the **canonical interlingua at
that joint**: lyrics are phonemized per language; every vowel
the phonemizer emits is then seated — or honestly reported
unseated — against the Nine; the seated vowel targets, with
their formant coordinates, are what the vocal module is tuned,
measured, and quality-checked against. One canon of nine vowel
targets, standing between any number of languages and any
number of renderers. The renderer may change (DiffSinger today,
an owned bank tomorrow); the vowel law does not.

**Row reasoning, one by one:**

- **Α /a/ (1).** Reasoning: the open vowel — jaw fully open,
  tongue low, F1 at its maximum; the vowel of greatest sonority
  and the easiest carrier of pitch at power, which is why it
  stands first in the canon and first in the mouth. Japanese
  **a** is the same /a/: exact seat, no offset. English **AA**
  /ɑ/ seats here with a small coordinate noted: /ɑ/ is low-
  *back*, Greek /a/ low-central — same height, a backness
  divergence of roughly one step on the F2 axis (≈1090 → ≈940
  Hz). Reported, not smoothed.
- **Ε /e/ (5).** Reasoning: the close-mid front vowel; Japanese
  **e** is /e/: exact seat. English **EY** /eɪ/ *begins* at /e/
  but does not stay — it glides toward /ɪ/, and /ɪ/ holds no
  station (see the offset list). EY is therefore **half-seated**:
  its onset is Ε's, its offset target is unseated ground. The
  Nine seats the ground the glide leaves from, not where it
  lands.
- **Η /ɛː/ (8).** Reasoning: the open-mid front vowel, Ε's lower
  neighbor — the canon separates /e/ and /ɛː/ by one height
  step, and English does the same: **EH** /ɛ/ seats here
  exactly in quality. One coordinate stands: Greek Η is *long*
  (ː), English EH is short — a duration offset, and duration in
  an SVS engine is a parameter (`ph_dur`), not a different
  vowel; the seat holds, the length is the engine's to set.
  Japanese has no /ɛ/: JA **e** seats at Ε alone, and the
  Ε/Η distinction is one Japanese does not phonemize — a
  JA-side OFFSET, coordinates: JA's front-mid space is one
  vowel wide where the canon is two.
- **Ι /i/ (10).** Reasoning: the close front vowel, F1 minimal,
  F2 maximal — the brightest steady state in the canon, the
  vowel a soprano's high line thins toward. Japanese **i** is
  /i/ and English **IY** is /i/: both exact, no offset. This is
  the table's cleanest row.
- **Ο /o/ (70).** Reasoning: the close-mid back rounded vowel;
  Japanese **o** is /o/: exact seat. English **OW** /oʊ/ begins
  here and glides toward /ʊ/, which holds no station — OW is
  **half-seated**, onset at Ο, offset target unseated, same
  disposition as EY at Ε.
- **Υ /y/ (400).** Reasoning: the surplus seat, and the
  table's most instructive row. /y/ is /i/'s tongue position
  with /u/'s lips — close, front, *rounded*. Neither Japanese
  nor English phonemizes it (it lives in French, German,
  Turkish, Mandarin *ü*). Acoustically it is no near-miss of
  /i/: the rounding lowers F3 and pulls F2 down (≈2290 →
  ≈2100 Hz), a timbre a listener hears at once. The Nine
  therefore holds **one vowel the house can sing that neither
  target language can say** — no lexical job exists for it in
  JA or EN text, and §V takes up its real employment: chant,
  vocalise, and non-lexical hooks, where a voice may go where
  words do not.
- **Ω /ɔː/ (800).** Reasoning: the open-mid back vowel, Ο's
  lower neighbor, mirroring the Ε/Η step on the back side of
  the space — the canon's symmetry is acoustic, not decorative:
  two height steps in front (Ε, Η), two in back (Ο, Ω), the
  extremes Α below and Ι above, Υ the rounded outlier. English
  **AO** /ɔ/ seats here with the same length coordinate as Η
  (Greek long, English short; duration is the engine's
  parameter). Japanese has no /ɔ/ — a JA-side OFFSET, mirror of
  the Η row: JA's back-mid space is one vowel wide (o at Ο)
  where the canon is two.

**The OFFSET list — English and Japanese vowels with no
station, coordinates reported, none forced:**

- **JA u** [ɯ] — high, back, *unrounded*. The canon's high
  seats are Ι (front, unrounded) and Υ (front, rounded); no
  high-back seat exists in the Nine at all. u stands between
  two absences: backness with no station, rounding with none.
  This is the single standing offset on the Japanese side —
  every Japanese lyric line carries it (う is among the
  commonest morae in the language), so the interlingua must
  carry it as a named offset target, defined by its formant
  coordinates (F1 ≈ 300, F2 ≈ 1390 Hz), not pretend it is Ι or
  Υ. Coordinates, not a verdict.
- **EN IH** /ɪ/ — the lax near-close front vowel: one height
  step below Ι, centralized toward the middle. Coordinates:
  between Ι and Ε on the height axis, inward on the F2 axis.
  It is also the landing target of EY and AY — both glides land
  on unseated ground.
- **EN AE** /æ/ — low-front, raised from /a/ toward /e/:
  between Α and Ε, off the front edge. No station.
- **EN UH** /ʊ/ and **EN UW** /u/ — the high-back pair, lax
  and tense. No station, for the same structural reason as JA
  u: the Nine has no high-back seat. (Boundary note, stated and
  left standing: in the earliest Greek, the ΟΥ graph once wrote
  a long /uː/; in the founder's canon ΟΥ functions as the

  history, not an amendment to his canon.)
- **EN AH** /ə, ʌ/ — the central vowel, schwa: the acoustic
  centroid of the whole vowel space, F1 ≈ 500, F2 ≈ 1400 Hz —
  the point equidistant from every station and seated at none.
  In zero-point terms it is the closest thing the mouth has to
  an origin with no axis. Unseated; coordinates reported.
- **EN ER** /ɚ/ — r-colored: a vowel with a consonantal

  consonant question, §IV, rather than seated or offset here.
- **EN AW** /aʊ/, **AY** /aɪ/, **OY** /ɔɪ/ — English's own

  the table whose *both* ends the canon governs (ΟΥ's end
  target being the named high-back coordinate above).

**Japanese length.** JA long vowels (ā ī ū ē ō) are duration,
not new qualities: the same five seats held twice as long. In
`.ds` terms this is `ph_dur`, the duration model's business —
the interlingua seats the quality and lets the engine carry
the length, the same disposition as the Η/Ω length offsets on
the English side. One law for both languages.

## §III — THE HYBRIDS AS GLIDES

The founder's doctrine for the two hybrids is his, stated in
his ruling and carried here as his words: ΕΙ and ΟΥ are
*"fluid hybrid vowels, both masculine and feminine at once."*
[FOUNDER] The desk adds no gloss to the doctrine. What the desk
supplies is the mechanism by which an engine performs a hybrid
— and the mechanism and the doctrine describe the same object
from two sides: **one note, two vowels, neither surrendered.**

**Engine specification [CONSTRUCTED].** In DiffSinger terms a
note carries `ph_num` phonemes; a hybrid is written as *one
note with `ph_num` = 2* — `ph_seq` […, e, i, …] under a single
`note_seq` entry, no slur break. The variance (duration) model
then divides the note between the two targets, and the acoustic
model renders the formant trajectory between them. The desk's
working parameters, offered as tunable construction, not law:

- **ΕΙ** — trajectory Ε /e/ → Ι /i/. Onset target held ≈65% of
  the note's duration; transition (F1 falling ≈512 → ≈270 Hz,
  F2 rising ≈1950 → ≈2290 Hz) across the final ≈35%. Both ends
  are stations of the Nine: the canon's only fully-seated front
  glide.
- **ΟΥ** — trajectory Ο /o/ → the high-back coordinate. Onset
  held ≈65%; transition across the final ≈35%, F1 falling
  ≈570 → ≈300 Hz, F2 falling ≈840 → ≈700 Hz toward the
  [u]-ward target. Its end is the named offset coordinate of
  §II (the seat the Nine does not hold), specified by formants
  rather than by station — the glide is governed at its start
  by a station and at its end by a coordinate, and the study
  reports that asymmetry rather than hiding it.

A hybrid sung at these settings is one phoneme-slot that is
two vowels in succession without a boundary — which is what a
diphthong is, in Greek, in English, and in the `.ds` file
alike. Where a language's own diphthong is half-seated (EY, OW,
AY, AW, OY), the interlingua writes it the same way — onset
station plus offset coordinate — so the canon's treatment of
its own hybrids becomes the template for every glide the
engine meets.

## §IV — THE CONSONANT QUESTION — OPEN FOR HIS PEN

The Nine governs vowels. Singing also needs consonants: JA's
k, s, t, n, h, m, y, r, w and moraic ん; EN's two dozen. No
ruling of the founder's assigns the consonants, and this study
decides nothing. The options, stated neutrally:

- **Option A — the Greek consonants.** His wider alphabet banks
  (the 777/correspondence tables) already carry the full Greek
  alphabet with its weighings. A full-Greek interlingua —
  consonants seated as the vowels are — would make the layer
  complete and doctrinally of one piece. Cost, stated plainly:
  Greek consonant phonetics do not cover either language's
  inventory (JA's moraic nasal, EN's /θ ð ʒ tʃ dʒ/ and the
  rhotic have no Greek seats), so a mapping with its own offset
  list — longer than §II's — would have to be built and ruled.
- **Option B — the Nine rules vowels alone.** Consonants stay
  language-native, emitted by each phonemizer (DIFFS JA, DIFFS
  EN) exactly as the engines already expect them, unseated and
  unweighed. The canon governs the part of the signal that
  carries pitch and timbre; consonants pass through as the
  edges they acoustically are.
- **Desk recommendation [CONSTRUCTED, labeled as such]:**
  Option B for the first build. The vowels are where the pitch
  lives and where the canon's law is complete; the consonant
  inventories are where the two languages differ most and where
  the phonemizers are already correct. Option A remains open
  entire as his doctrinal call — if he rules the consonants
  into the Greek banks, the interlingua extends; nothing in
  this study's vowel layer would need to change.

The question stands open. ER /ɚ/ (§II) waits on it, its rhotic
being a consonant wearing a vowel's duration.

## §V — SECOND USE: CHANT AND VOCALISE

A vowel canon earns its keep a second way: **lines with no
words at all.**

- **Vocalise hooks.** A hook sung on pure vowels — one station
  held, or a hybrid glide, across a melodic line — needs no
  lyric, no phonemizer, no language at all. The Nine is the
  complete palette for such lines: seven steady colors and two
  glides, each with formant coordinates the module can be
  tuned and measured against. Υ /y/, unemployed by JA and EN
  text, is fully employed here — the surplus seat is the
  vocalise seat, the vowel neither language can say and any
  voice can sing.
- **The trance-aid bed.** The house's Mugetsu trance aid
  carries its invocation in actual Japanese over a binaural
  pair the house installs by hand. The invocation's *text*
  stays Japanese and exactly as filed; but a vowel bed beneath
  or between its lines —
  sustained stations and ΟΥ/ΕΙ glides at low level — is a
  design the Nine now specifies exactly: which vowels, in
  which order, with which trajectories, instead of an
  unspecified hum. [CONSTRUCTED as design; the invocation
  itself is the founder's filed text and is not altered.]
- **Beside the Sigma practice.** The house already keeps a
  pitch-side canon: the Sigma solfège ladder (UT 396, RE 417,
  MI 528, FA 639, SOL 741, LA 852) and the Sigma binaural pair
  installed under its masters. The Nine is the **timbre-side**
  canon of the same practice: Sigma governs which frequencies
  underlie the work; the Nine governs which vowel colors the
  voice itself carries. Pitch from the ladder and the score,
  color from the canon — two coordinate systems, each reported
  in its own terms, neither collapsed into the other. That
  division is this study's construction, labeled as such; no
  doctrine is minted by it.

### 2. VV's acoustic face

As sound, VV/W is **/w/** — the labial-velar glide, which is
the consonantal form of /u/: the same lips rounded, the same
tongue-back raised, held as a glide instead of a steady state.
And in Welsh vowel service the same letter does full vowel
duty, a /u/-family vowel — **/ʊ/** in *cwm*. One letter, both
offices, in the thesis's own reasoning, quoted: *"one
function, doubled form, vowel or consonant decided by the
reading tongue."* [The office-doubling is the founder's
clause, FOUNDER; the /w/ and /ʊ/ values are ATTESTED

In engine terms: /w/ and /u/ are a single articulatory target
in two durations — the glide and the steady state of one
high-back gesture. The study's table, as seated, held no
letter for that gesture at all. **VV is that letter.**
